Laboratory of Pharmaceutical Sciences

Pharmacokinetics · Drug metabolism · Drug transport

We study how drugs are absorbed, distributed, metabolized and excreted, and how these processes change with disease, drug–drug interactions and individual differences. We connect animal and in vitro experiments, LC-MS/MS bioanalysis and quantitative modeling to drug discovery and the safe use of medicines.

Research

  • Pharmacokinetics of drug candidates and disease models

    We characterize the systemic exposure of new drug candidates and marketed drugs, in healthy animals and in disease models, and identify what governs it.

  • Drug metabolism and metabolites

    We study how drugs are metabolized, which metabolites form, and how metabolism shapes exposure and safety, supported by LC-MS/MS metabolite analysis.

  • Drug transporters and drug–drug interactions

    Co-prescribed drugs often compete for the same enzymes and transporters. We examine enzyme- and transporter-mediated interactions to explain changes in efficacy and adverse effects.

  • Quantitative modeling and prediction

    We use pharmacokinetic, physiologically based (PBPK) and population (nonlinear mixed-effects) models, and machine learning, to interpret data and predict exposure in situations that were not tested.

From a question to a dosing rationale

Six steps connect a research question to the use of a medicine.

  1. Design. Define the question, choose an in vitro system or animal model, and plan dosing and sampling.
  2. Experiment. In vitro metabolism and transport assays; pharmacokinetic studies in rodents, including disease models.
  3. Bioanalysis. Develop and validate LC-MS/MS methods to measure drugs and metabolites in biological samples.
  4. PK analysis. Derive exposure and kinetic parameters (AUC, Cmax, half-life, clearance) from concentration–time data.
  5. Modeling. Compartmental, population and physiologically based models explain variability and predict untested scenarios such as drug–drug interactions.
  6. Translate. Turn results into dosing rationale, interaction risk and safety considerations.
