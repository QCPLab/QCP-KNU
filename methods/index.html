Methods you can explore

Two interactive explainers built on simulated patients and public molecule structures show how methods used in modern pharmacology work. They are teaching examples, not results from patients or from our experiments.

The patients are simulated. In the second deep-learning example the molecules are real public structures, and their logP is calculated by software, not measured. The calculations run in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere.

How a population PK model is fitted

People differ in how fast they clear a drug. A nonlinear mixed-effects (NLME) model describes the typical values and the spread between individuals at once. The optimiser starts from poor guesses and improves them step by step; here you can move through every step.

  1. Choose a sampling scenario: rich, moderate or sparse.
  2. Press play, or drag the slider through the optimiser's iterations.
  3. Watch the fitted curves, the parameter estimates and the objective function change.
  4. Select a subject to compare its data with its individual fit.

Simulated data from 30 virtual subjects and a single model. The estimator is a from-scratch implementation of first-order conditional estimation with interaction (FOCE-I). It illustrates the idea and is not a substitute for validated software.

How a neural network computes

A neural network is a chain of simple calculations: weighted sums followed by a non-linearity. Training changes the weights to reduce the error. Run the forward pass, inspect the gradients of the backward pass, and follow message passing across a real drug molecule.

Teaching examples with small models. The neural network learns a known formula from synthetic patients. The graph network learns to reproduce the Crippen logP that RDKit calculates (a calculated value, not a measurement) from about 4,600 public structures (NCI Open Database, as shipped with RDKit). They show the mechanics and are not validated for prediction.
